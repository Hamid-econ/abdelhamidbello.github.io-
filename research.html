Working papers

  1. Intergenerational Transmission of Inequalities: Are Health Inequalities at Birth the Missing Link?
    Abdel-Hamid Bello, Maripier Isabelle, and Guy Lacroix. Under review.
    Working paper
    Abstract

    This paper studies whether poor health at birth contributes to the persistence of economic inequality across generations. Using linked Canadian administrative data covering two birth cohorts, we document the intergenerational transmission of low birth weight and examine how health at birth shapes the probability of moving above parental income rank. Sibling comparisons indicate that low birth weight reduces upward mobility, while subsequent childhood health shocks explain part, but not all, of this relationship.

Work in progress

  • Breaking the Odds: Improving Childhood Socio-Emotional Skills to Raise Intergenerational Income Mobility.
    Yann Algan, Sylvana Côté, Maripier Isabelle, and Abdel-Hamid Bello.
    Abstract

    This project studies whether an early-childhood intervention targeting socio-emotional skills can affect long-run intergenerational income mobility. We link the Montreal Longitudinal Experimental Study to administrative tax records and compare treated boys with randomized controls, focusing on adult income ranks relative to parental income.

  • Early-childhood interventions and adult economic mobility.
